Course Introduction

Build visual thinking for real sport business decisions

This course combines visual theory, practical Tableau workflows and guided analysis. Students learn to select the right visual form, communicate evidence responsibly and turn raw datasets into decisions that stakeholders can understand and use.

How the Course Works

01

Learn

Explore essential data visualisation principles, chart selection and Tableau techniques.

02

Practise

Apply each concept through guided activities, independent exercises and interpretation tasks.

03

Discuss

Compare visual decisions, critique dashboards and contribute to evidence-based class debates.

04

Apply

Bring together each block of skills in practical workshops and a final Tableau project.

Course Structure

Three blocks, each ending in applied practice

The course is organised around a repeated learning rhythm: four sessions build the concepts and Tableau skills, then a workshop asks students to apply them in a structured analytical challenge.

Block 1

Visual Foundations and Tableau Building Blocks

Students move from visual reasoning to their first Tableau workflows: connecting data, encoding information, choosing charts and preparing reliable data.

Key topics

  • Good and misleading charts

Learning outcome: explain how visual context turns data into decisions.

Preparing Trustworthy Data Models

View overview Hide overview ↓

Prepare, model and test data before Workshop 1.

This session shows why a well-designed chart can still be wrong if the data model is weak. Students inspect raw Sport Business files, clean fields, identify keys and grain, choose joins, unions or relationships, and test for duplicated rows or inflated totals.

Key topics

  • Raw-file triage
  • Cleaning and validation
  • Joins, unions and relationships
  • Keys, grain and cardinality

Learning outcome: build and publish a tested Tableau data model that supports trustworthy visuals.

How we'll learn

This course is designed to be active.

This is not a course where you simply watch someone build dashboards. You will be asked to think, answer, test ideas and build throughout the class.

Camera policy This is an interactive online course, and active presence is part of the learning experience.

Students are expected to keep their camera on during class. Unless there is a strictly justified reason communicated to the instructor, students who keep their camera off may be removed from the session.

Attendance requirement Students are expected to attend at least 80% of the scheduled teaching sessions.

Across the 15 course meetings, this means attending at least 12 sessions.

15Total meetings
80%Minimum attendance
12Sessions required
CHAT

Interact

Expect questions throughout the session: chat responses, options, predictions, visual critiques, interpretations and reasoning.

The objective is not to catch you out. It is to make the learning active.

BUILD

Practice

Most concepts move quickly from explanation to application: Tableau exercises, guided activities, business scenarios and hands-on challenges.

We will not wait until the end of the course to practise.

QUIZ

Check

End-of-session Kahoot. Every normal teaching session ends with a short Kahoot based only on what we covered that session.

Short and focused. Straightforward if the key ideas are clear.

TASK

Consolidate

Some sessions may include a small take-home practice activity to reinforce what was already taught in class.

You should not need to learn a completely new concept on your own to complete it.

Participation

You will frequently be asked: “What do you think?”, “Why?”, “Compared with what?” and “What would you do next?”

Kahoot checks

Questions may ask you to identify a concept, interpret a scenario, choose an analytical decision, recognise a mistake or apply something just practised.

Online, but active

Expect chat, reactions, Tableau, short challenges, live demonstrations and collaborative work during workshops.

REST

5-minute reset

Around the middle of a normal teaching session, we will take a short break. Stand up, get some water, look somewhere that is not a dashboard, and come back ready for round two.

After enough charts, questions and Tableau, attention starts negotiating its resignation.

TEAM

Workshops are different.

Workshops are practical working sessions with little or no new teaching. You will work in pairs, usually formed or confirmed during the session immediately before the workshop, and each pair will work independently in its own Teams call.

01Pair up

Before leaving the previous session, know your partner, group and where to find your Teams link.

02Join your room

Each pair uses a dedicated Teams call for screen share, discussion and Tableau work.

03Work together

Complete the challenge as a team, divide tasks where useful and switch roles.

04Checkpoint

The instructor rotates between calls to review progress, coach and challenge reasoning.

05Explain

Either student may be asked to explain the question, approach, Tableau work or finding.

2X

Both means both.

Both members of the pair are responsible for the complete solution. One analyst building while the other watches is not pair work.

You can divide the work and help each other, but both students should understand the full solution by the end.

Direct Class Participation — 10%

Students receive credit for meaningful contributions made during live sessions. Participation is observed and recorded throughout the course, with a maximum of 3 points per session so quality, relevance and clarity matter more than speaking often.

Evidence levelPointsWhat it means
Strong contribution3Well-reasoned reflection, correct use of concepts, justified decisions, professional case connection or a valuable interpretation.
Relevant contribution2Correct answer, relevant question, constructive participation, basic decision explanation or exercise presentation.
Basic contribution1Brief participation, a valid but undeveloped idea, collaboration in an activity or a partial answer.
No evidence0No recorded evidence for that activity or session. This is not a personality judgement or a penalty.

Session Kahoots — 10%

At the beginning of each session, students complete a short Kahoot reviewing the main concepts from the previous session. The academic focus is participation, correction and consistency, not ranking speed.

  • Completion: 40% of each Kahoot score.
  • Accuracy: 60% of each Kahoot score.
  • The Kahoot grade is based on the average across eligible session Kahoots.
  • The lowest Kahoot score may be excluded from the final average.

Learning Engagement — 5%

Learning Engagement recognises consistent involvement in short reflective, collaborative and practical activities completed throughout the course. Selected activities use simple evidence levels: Completed, Partially completed or Not completed.

  • Examples include chart choice defence, spot the problem, one-minute insight, think-pair-share, Tableau demonstration, peer review, exit ticket and decision challenge.
  • Only selected activities are evaluated; the course does not record every click or every web interaction.
