S14-00 · Opening story clinic

A correct dashboard can still fail if the audience cannot find the point.

Data dumpEverything is visible

The audience must decide what matters without guidance.

Chart paradeEvery view is interesting

The charts do not build one connected argument.

Generic conclusion“Performance should improve”

The recommendation names no owner, action or success measure.

Hidden stateFilters change the story silently

The viewer cannot tell which subset supports the claim.

Find the storytelling problem

A dashboard contains six polished charts. The title says “Sales Analysis”, no insight is highlighted and the conclusion says “Monitor performance closely”.

Start with one decision question

“Is growth stable enough to support the next investment, and which part of the portfolio creates the risk?”

A useful story narrows the decision before it expands the evidence.

S14-01 · The analytical story spine

Each section should change what the audience understands or decides.

1 · SignalWhat changed?

Name the relevant movement, size and period.

Growth slowed from 11% to 3% in the latest quarter.
2 · DiagnosisWhere and why?

Locate the segment, category or mechanism.

Furniture discounts increased while its profit ratio fell.
3 · DecisionWhat should happen?

State an action within the audience's control.

Review discount rules for loss-making Furniture sub-categories.
4 · ProofHow will we know?

Define owner, timing and success measure.

Commercial team tracks monthly profit ratio against the 12% target.

S14-02 · Evidence and annotation

The chart should prove the claim with the least possible decoding.

ClaimGrowth is becoming less stable

Needs direction and variation over time.

Best evidenceTime series + 6-month moving average

Shows the raw movement and the underlying pattern.

AnnotationMark the break in trend

Explain the event or segment contribution without covering the data.

LimitTrend is descriptive

Do not claim causation without supporting evidence.

Monthly profitDiscount increase
Profit crosses below target

Annotation hierarchy

  1. Use a takeaway title that states the finding.
  2. Label the relevant point or range.
  3. Add a short explanation only when the evidence cannot speak alone.
  4. Keep definitions and caveats available without competing with the insight.

Evidence match

Your claim is “Revenue is concentrated in a small number of customers.” Which evidence best supports it?

One claim, one primary proof

Secondary views may add context, but the audience should know which chart carries the argument.

S14-03 · Tableau Story Points

A Story Point should advance the analysis, not repeat the dashboard.

Story 1Growth Analysis

Establish the trajectory and stability of change.

  • Insight 1: direction and pace
  • Insight 2: volatility or break point
  • Recommendation: response to growth pattern
Story 2Structural Analysis

Explain concentration, distribution and outliers.

  • Insight 1: concentration
  • Insight 2: variability or risk
  • Recommendation: portfolio action
Story 3Strategic Decision

Connect rank, relationships and segments.

  • Insight 1: position or movement
  • Insight 2: opportunity cluster
  • Recommendation: prioritised decision

Hands-on · Build a three-point story

Use captions as analytical headlines

  1. Create a new Story using the final dashboard size.
  2. Add the Growth dashboard as the first point.
  3. Capture the filter state that supports the insight.
  4. Write a caption that states the finding, not the chart name.
  5. Duplicate only when the state changes the argument.
  6. Add Structural and Strategic points in decision order.
Weak caption

“Dashboard 1 – Sales”

Stronger caption

“Growth slowed after June as Furniture profit deteriorated”

Can the caption stand alone? Does the displayed state prove it? Does the next point answer the question this point creates?

S14-04 · Dashboard refinement

Refinement removes anything that competes with the decision.

HierarchyCan users find the status first?

Title, KPI state, primary evidence, diagnosis and detail should read in that order.

ConsistencyDo repeated meanings look the same?

Keep formats, colour rules, labels and interaction states stable.

AccessibilityDoes meaning survive without colour?

Add direct labels, sufficient contrast and clear focus states.

DensityDoes every object earn space?

Remove duplicated legends, decorative marks and unused controls.

Before12 objects · 7 colours · 5 legends

The viewer searches for the message.

After1 headline · 1 primary view · detail on demand

The evidence and action become visible.

Accessibility check

Profit status uses only red and green circles with no labels. What is the strongest correction?

AccuracyReconcile totals and grains

Check table calculations after filters and actions.

StateTest select, clear and reset

Titles and captions must reflect active filters.

SizeTest the delivery viewport

No clipped labels, scroll traps or overlapping objects.

PerformanceRecord realistic load time

Use the full data and typical interactions.

Self-guided rehearsal

Deliver a three-minute executive walkthrough

  1. 30 seconds: state the decision question and current status.
  2. 60 seconds: show the strongest evidence and explain the mechanism.
  3. 45 seconds: demonstrate one interaction that reveals diagnosis.
  4. 30 seconds: give the recommendation, owner and success measure.
  5. 15 seconds: state one limitation or next validation step.

S14-07 · Final decision check

The story succeeds when evidence, interaction and recommendation support one decision.

QuestionIs the decision specific?

One audience, one decision context.

ClaimIs the finding measurable?

Measure, direction, size and period.

EvidenceDoes the displayed state prove it?

Appropriate chart, grain and comparison.

SequenceDoes each point advance the reasoning?

Signal, diagnosis, action and validation.

ExperienceCan the user recover context?

Clear filters, reset and navigation.

TrustCan someone verify the work?

Definitions, limitations and downloadable workbook.
